Course Overview

What is Mechanical AutoCAD?

Mechanical AutoCAD is the industry's most widely used software for creating precise 2D mechanical drawings and engineering documentation. From machine component design to complete manufacturing assemblies, this course equips you with comprehensive skills to produce professional-grade engineering drawings that meet international standards and industrial requirements.

Mechanical Drafting

Master precision 2D drafting techniques used in mechanical engineering, including orthographic projections, section views, and auxiliary views for complex machine parts.

Component Design

Design individual machine components including gears, shafts, bearings, flanges, and fasteners with accurate dimensioning and geometric tolerancing specifications.

Manufacturing Drawings

Create complete manufacturing drawings with all required specifications including material callouts, surface finish symbols, and production annotations that shops need.

Assembly Drawings

Develop detailed assembly drawings showing how components fit together, complete with exploded views, bill of materials, and part identification balloons for production.

Drafting Techniques

Mechanical Drafting Techniques

Our Mechanical AutoCAD training focuses on developing precision drafting skills that are directly applicable in manufacturing and engineering environments. You will learn the fundamentals of technical drawing language understood globally by engineers and manufacturers.

  • Orthographic Projection — First and third angle projection for complete multi-view drawings with correct line types and standards.
  • Section Views — Full, half, revolved, and removed sections to clearly show internal features of complex components.
  • Auxiliary Views — True shape views of inclined surfaces that cannot be shown in standard principal views.
  • Detail Views — Enlarged views for small or complex features requiring additional clarity and annotation.
  • GD&T Symbols — Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing symbols applied correctly to ensure functional fits and interchangeability.
Component Design

Machine Component Design

Understanding how to design individual mechanical components is the foundation of any engineering career. Our course covers the complete design documentation process for all common machine elements used across manufacturing industries.

  • Shafts & Couplings — Design and detail transmission shafts, keys, keyways, and various coupling types for power transmission systems.
  • Gears & Sprockets — Technical drawings for spur, helical, bevel gears and chain sprockets with proper tooth profile representation.
  • Bearings & Housings — Document bearing selections, fits, and housing designs for rotating machinery applications.
  • Fasteners & Joints — Represent threaded fasteners, welded joints, and brazed connections with correct conventional symbols.
  • Piping & Flanges — Create pipe layout drawings and flange detail drawings for industrial piping systems.
Manufacturing Drawings

Production-Ready Manufacturing Drawings

Manufacturing drawings are the communication bridge between design engineers and the production floor. You will learn to create complete, unambiguous drawings that contain all information required for machining, fabrication, and quality inspection.

  • Title Block & Revision Control — Professional title blocks with complete drawing information, tolerances, and revision history management.
  • Dimensional Tolerancing — Limit and fit systems including clearance, interference, and transition fits for mating components.
  • Surface Finish Symbols — Surface texture symbols and roughness values required for machined surfaces and functional areas.
  • Material & Process Callouts — Material specifications, heat treatment notes, and manufacturing process annotations on drawings.
  • ISO & ASME Standards — Apply international drawing standards ensuring drawings are understood worldwide in global manufacturing environments.
Assembly Drawings

Complete Assembly Drawings

Assembly drawings show the complete picture of how individual components combine into functional mechanical systems. Mastering assembly documentation is essential for any engineer working in product design, maintenance, or manufacturing planning.

  • General Assembly Views — Complete assembly showing all components in their final assembled position with proper conventions.
  • Exploded View Drawings — Isometric exploded views clearly showing component sequence and assembly relationships for maintenance.
  • Bill of Materials (BOM) — Structured parts lists with item numbers, descriptions, quantities, material grades, and standards.
  • Balloon Callouts — Item number balloons linked to BOM for clear identification of each component in the assembly.
  • Sub-Assembly Documentation — Managing complex assemblies by organizing into sub-assemblies with proper hierarchy and cross-referencing.

Fabrication & Tolerancing

Fabrication Drawings & Dimensioning

Fabrication drawings bridge design intent with actual production. Learning proper dimensioning and tolerancing techniques ensures your designs can be manufactured accurately and economically, reducing costly rework and production errors.

  • Weld Symbol Representation — Standard AWS and ISO welding symbols on structural and fabricated components to communicate joint requirements to welders.
  • Sheet Metal Drawings — Flat pattern layouts, bend notes, K-factor calculations, and forming specifications for sheet metal fabrication work.
  • Tolerance Stack-Up Analysis — Understanding how dimensional tolerances accumulate in assemblies and how to allocate tolerances to ensure proper function.
  • Casting & Forging Drawings — Draft angles, parting lines, core prints, and machining allowances for cast and forged components in production.
Documentation

Engineering Documentation

Professional engineering documentation goes beyond individual drawings. Complete product documentation packages are essential for manufacturing, quality control, maintenance, and regulatory compliance in modern engineering organizations.

  • Drawing Management — Numbering systems, revision control, document hierarchy, and release procedures for engineering drawing packages.
  • Technical Specifications — Writing and incorporating material specifications, process specifications, and quality standards into drawing packages.
  • Engineering Change Orders — Proper procedures for modifying released drawings while maintaining design history and traceability records.
  • Plot Plan & Layout — Plant layout drawings showing equipment arrangement, access routes, and maintenance clearances for industrial facilities.
